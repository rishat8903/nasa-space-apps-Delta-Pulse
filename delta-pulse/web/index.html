RADAR SCATTERING PHYSICS

01 Specular Reflection (Open Water)

Mechanism: Smooth water acts as a specular mirror for 24 cm microwaves, reflecting energy away from the side-looking radar antenna (33°–47° incidence angle).

NISAR Signature: Very low backscatter in both channels (γ⁰HH ≈ −16 to −24 dB, γ⁰HV ≈ −22 to −28 dB). Appears deep blue/black in false-color composites.

02 Dihedral Double-Bounce (Flooded Stems & Settlements)

Mechanism: L-band waves penetrate upper foliage, reflect off the horizontal water surface onto vertical rice/wetland stems (or building walls), and return strongly toward the radar.

NISAR Signature: Strong co-polarized surge (γ⁰HH > −7.5 dB) with a large polarization difference (HH − HV > 6.5 dB). Highlighted in emerald green on our Flood Dynamics map.

03 Volume Depolarization (Crop & Forest Canopy)

Mechanism: Multiple scattering inside randomly oriented leaves, twigs, and crop stems depolarizes the radar wave, converting horizontal transmit pulses (H) into vertical receive echoes (V).

NISAR Signature: Elevated cross-pol γ⁰HV (−14 to −10 dB) and high HV/HH ratio. Drives our unsupervised paddy phenology clustering across cropland.

Physical Drivers Behind the 4 Radar Signatures

In Bangladesh's floodplain and haor agro-ecosystems, agricultural fields experience dramatic moisture and structural shifts between June and November:

  • Pondering / Agronomic Flooding: Prior to T. Aman transplanting—or when monsoon waters overtop low-lying boro fields—smooth water surfaces cause a sharp drop in both γ⁰HH and γ⁰HV (−18 to −22 dB).
  • Vegetative Tillering & Stem Elongation: As rice tillers emerge above standing water, 24 cm L-band waves experience stem-water dihedral double-bounce (γ⁰HH spikes) followed by progressive cross-pol volume scattering (γ⁰HV rises by +4 to +6 dB).
  • Senescence & Post-Monsoon Recession: As water drains in October–November and crops mature/harvest, soil roughness and dry stubble dominate the return.

⚠️ Scientific Honesty: Why This is a Physical Hypothesis

In accordance with NASA remote sensing standards, our K=4 trajectory map is presented explicitly as an unsupervised radar backscatter hypothesis rather than a supervised crop-type census:

  • Single-Season Provisional Window: NISAR entered its Provisional Calibrated science phase on June 17, 2026. The 12-pass window (2026-06-18 to 2026-11-09) captures the Kharif-II / Aman monsoon season, whereas a complete Boro–Aus–Aman cycle requires a full 12-month archive.
  • No Field-Survey Ground Truth: While strictly constrained to real ESA WorldCover 10m Cropland (class 40), distinguishing specific rice cultivars from jute or wet fallow requires in-situ DAE plot surveys.
